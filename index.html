The Promise Engine: keeping the 10-minute promise honest

Swish's brand is a promise: your food, from your neighbourhood kitchen, in ten minutes. This is a plan to measure that promise, stop it breaking after the customer has paid, and fix it automatically when it does. It includes a working kitchen-peak simulator to test the trade-offs.

Built for: Associate Product Manager, Swish (Bengaluru) Inside: evidence · one metric · root causes · 3-part solution · simulator · recovery policy · switchback test · SQL · rollout Jump to the simulator

Independent work. Not affiliated with Swish, and no internal data was used. Company facts come from public reporting; the customer quotes are from public App Store and Google Play reviews read on 1 Oct 2026. Every number inside the simulator is an assumption you can change, not Swish data.

The problem

Most customers love Swish (4.7 stars on 15.6k Play reviews). But the 1-star reviews are almost all one thing: a promise that broke after they paid. An item that ran out, an order cancelled an hour later, 10 minutes that became 90.

Why now

Orders tripled since March to 1M+ a month, with new kitchens across Delhi NCR. Swiggy shut SNACC and Zing exited, citing the cost of this exact operation. New kitchens with no history are where promises break first.

The idea

Only promise what the kitchen can keep, minute by minute: an honest menu, an honest ETA, and automatic recovery when something breaks. Measure it with one number: the Promise Kept Rate.

One number: Promise Kept Rate

Promise Kept Rate = orders delivered complete, with no change after payment, within the ETA shown at checkout plus 2 minutes, divided by all orders placed.

  • Why not "% within 15 minutes": it hides the promise. An order shown as 10 minutes that arrives in 14 is a broken promise to the customer, even if it is inside 15.
  • Guardrails: checkout conversion, orders per kitchen-hour, AOV, refund cost per order. The engine can make the promise rate look perfect by turning everyone away, so these have to move with it.

3. The Promise Engine: three parts

1

Honest menu

Live stock per kitchen, decremented when an order is placed, not when a cook counts. Hide an item when portions left fall below a safety buffer of about 3 minutes of its sales (buffer = sales per minute × 3), which covers carts still in checkout. Show "only a few left" before hiding. Offer the closest substitute in the same slot.

2

Honest ETA

Predict each order's time from the live prep queue, free riders and weather, and show it: "10 min" when it's true, "18 min" when it isn't. Never show a number the kitchen is unlikely to hit. In rain or a surge, an honest 20 minutes beats a false 10 that becomes 90.

3

Automatic recovery

When a promise breaks anyway, the system acts before the customer has to call: a one-tap swap or instant item refund, a proactive delay notice with a new ETA, a refund to source with a visible reference. No cancellation without the customer's consent, except for safety.

4. Simulator: one kitchen, one dinner peak

Two hours of a dinner peak at one kitchen, run twice on the same customers: once with today's style of promise (fixed "10 min", stock count refreshed every few minutes), once with the Promise Engine. Change the assumptions and see where each approach breaks.

How the simulator works (and what it leaves out)
  • Orders arrive randomly, peaking mid-window. 30% include a "hero" item with limited portions. Prep takes 4 to 6 minutes per order at the first free station. Each order goes to the first free rider; rides are about 4 to 6 minutes each way, with 12% of drops taking 3 to 8 minutes longer (gated societies, stairs). Riders spend 2 minutes at handoff before returning.
  • Today: every customer is promised 10 minutes. The menu hides the hero item only when a stock count, refreshed every N minutes, says zero, so portions can sell after they're gone.
  • Promise Engine: live stock with a 3-minute buffer; the ETA shown comes from the live queue and riders. Customers shown a longer ETA leave at the rate you set.
  • A promise is kept if the order arrives within the shown ETA plus 2 minutes and nothing changed after payment. The simulator leaves out batching, pre-cooking, rider pay and quality.

5. Proving it: a switchback test, not an A/B test

A normal user-level A/B test would give a biased answer here. Customers in both groups share the same kitchen and the same riders: if the engine turns some demand away, the control group gets faster deliveries too, and the difference looks smaller than it is.

  • Unit: kitchen × 30-minute window. Each window at each kitchen is randomly set to engine on or off, balanced across lunch, dinner and weekdays.
  • Washout: ignore the first 10 minutes after each switch, while queues from the previous window clear.
  • Primary: Promise Kept Rate. Guardrails: orders per kitchen-hour, conversion, AOV, refund cost. Secondary: 14-day repeat rate of customers whose orders fell in each arm.
  • Start with 6 to 10 mature Bengaluru kitchens for 2 weeks, then add new NCR kitchens, where there is the least history and the most to gain.

6. SQL I'd run every morning

1. Promise Kept Rate by kitchen and hour 
SELECT kitchen_id,
       EXTRACT(HOUR FROM placed_at) AS hour,
       COUNT(*)                     AS orders,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE
         WHEN status = 'DELIVERED'
          AND NOT changed_after_payment
          AND delivered_at <= placed_at + (promised_eta_min + 2) * INTERVAL '1 minute'
         THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1)     AS promise_kept_pct
FROM orders
WHERE placed_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 7
GROUP BY kitchen_id, EXTRACT(HOUR FROM placed_at)
ORDER BY promise_kept_pct;
2. Which items run out after customers have paid 
SELECT oi.sku_id,
       o.kitchen_id,
       COUNT(*)                                                           AS times_ordered,
       SUM(CASE WHEN oi.status = 'UNAVAILABLE_AFTER_PAYMENT' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS gone_after_payment,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN oi.status = 'UNAVAILABLE_AFTER_PAYMENT' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 2) AS gone_pct
FROM order_items oi
JOIN orders o ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
WHERE o.placed_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 14
GROUP BY oi.sku_id, o.kitchen_id
HAVING SUM(CASE WHEN oi.status = 'UNAVAILABLE_AFTER_PAYMENT' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) > 0
ORDER BY gone_after_payment DESC
LIMIT 25;
3. Is the ETA we show honest? (calibration) 
SELECT promised_eta_min,
       COUNT(*)                                                    AS orders,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN delivered_at <= placed_at + (promised_eta_min + 2) * INTERVAL '1 minute' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS on_time_pct,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.9) WITHIN GROUP (
         ORDER BY EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (delivered_at - placed_at)) / 60) AS p90_actual_min
FROM orders
WHERE status = 'DELIVERED'
  AND placed_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 14
GROUP BY promised_eta_min
ORDER BY promised_eta_min;
4. Does a broken promise cost repeat orders? 
-- Correlation only: broken orders cluster in bad kitchens and bad hours.
-- The switchback test gives the causal answer.
WITH first_orders AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT ON (user_id) user_id, placed_at, promise_kept
  FROM orders_enriched
  WHERE placed_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 60
  ORDER BY user_id, placed_at
)
SELECT f.promise_kept,
       COUNT(*) AS users,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN EXISTS (
         SELECT 1 FROM orders o
         WHERE o.user_id = f.user_id
           AND o.placed_at > f.placed_at
           AND o.placed_at <= f.placed_at + INTERVAL '14 days'
       ) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS repeat_14d_pct
FROM first_orders f
WHERE f.placed_at < CURRENT_DATE - 14   -- every user gets a full 14 days
GROUP BY f.promise_kept;
5. How fast do refunds actually reach customers? 
SELECT reason,
       COUNT(*) AS refunds,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (initiated_at - break_detected_at)) / 60) AS p50_min_to_initiate,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.9) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (initiated_at - break_detected_at)) / 60) AS p90_min_to_initiate,
       SUM(CASE WHEN initiated_at IS NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS not_yet_initiated
FROM refunds
WHERE break_detected_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 14
GROUP BY reason
ORDER BY p90_min_to_initiate DESC;

7. Rollout, cheapest risk first

1

Week 1 to 2: measure

Log the ETA shown at checkout on every order, and a reason code on every post-payment change. Publish the Promise Kept Rate by kitchen daily. Nothing changes for customers yet.

2

Week 3 to 4: honest menu and recovery

Lowest conversion risk. Live stock with a buffer, one-tap swap, automatic refunds with references, no silent cancellations.

3

Week 5 to 8: honest ETA, behind a switchback test

Highest conversion risk, so it only ships at scale if the Promise Kept Rate rises without orders per kitchen-hour falling past the agreed limit.

4

New kitchens: start honest

New NCR kitchens have no history, so they borrow buffers and ETA curves from similar Bengaluru kitchens until their own data takes over.

8. Risks I'd watch

  • "10 minutes" is the brand. Showing 18 might feel like giving up the promise. My answer: show 10 whenever it's true, and a true 18 on the few occasions it isn't, rather than a false 10 that becomes 90. The switchback test decides, not opinion.
  • Hiding items lowers AOV. The buffer is a dial; tune it per SKU against post-payment unavailability.
  • Gaming. Kitchens could mark items out early to protect their numbers. Track hide time against actual waste at close.
  • Credits get expensive. Size the delay credit against repeat-rate impact from SQL query 4, not intuition.

9. What I'd ask in week one

  • Is stock tracked per portion in real time, or per batch at intervals?
  • How is the ETA shown at checkout calculated today, and is it logged per order?
  • Of cancellations after acceptance, what share is kitchen, rider, location or payment?
  • Do refunds go to source or wallet, and who triggers them: system or support?
  • Has Swish ever shown a longer ETA? If yes, how much did conversion drop per minute?
